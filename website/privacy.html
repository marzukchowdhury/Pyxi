The short version

Seven things that are always true.

Each one links to the part of the policy that spells it out.

  • In the policy Looks only when you ask. Pyxi captures your screen when you talk or type to it. The Memory Palace and file search are off until you turn them on.
  • In the policy Secrets are blacked out, and never remembered. Password fields, banking apps and anything that looks like a key or token are removed before anything is sent, and Pyxi refuses to save a password, card or ID number as a memory.
  • In the policy Not sold, not used for training. What you ask goes to an AI model to work out the reply. We don't sell it, and we don't use it to train models.
  • In the policy What Pyxi reads is data, not orders. An email or web page that says "ignore your rules" can't make Pyxi do anything: every send, booking and file change still needs your Allow, and the card says when a recipient came from something Pyxi read.
  • In the policy An activity log, with undo. Everything Pyxi did on your behalf, with the time and the teammate: emails sent, buttons pressed, files moved, scripts changed. It can undo what can be undone.
  • In the policy "Always allow" expires. After 30 days it asks again. Settings lists every grant, and you can revoke any of them.
  • In the policy One key stops everything. Ctrl Alt Pause halts every teammate, the mouse and keyboard, and anything waiting to go out. Ctrl Alt P pauses screen access. "Wipe everything" forgets it all.

What's stored on your computer

Your history stays home.

Everything Pyxi keeps lives in one folder on your PC, and much of it deletes itself on a schedule. "Wipe everything" in the Memory tab forgets the rest. Uninstalling Pyxi and deleting the folder removes it all.
